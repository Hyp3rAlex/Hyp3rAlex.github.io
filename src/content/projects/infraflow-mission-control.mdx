## Summary

InfraFlow Mission Control is an independent engineering demonstrator for infrastructure demand management: intake, assessment against budget and team capacity, a recorded human decision, and management reporting. It runs on entirely synthetic data for a fictional organisation, "Orion Systems Engineering", and is not deployed anywhere.

The core is an offline Python pipeline. A seeded generator writes nine CSV tables. The pipeline loads them without discarding anything, reconciles finance and capacity, scores each demand, evaluates four decision gates and builds an exception register. From one shared context it generates 13 Markdown reports, a facts JSON, two Excel workbooks, a five-slide deck and 12 Confluence page sources.

Two properties carry the design. Bad data is reported and given an owner: six critical exceptions are injected on purpose, so every output is marked PROVISIONAL. And everything is reproducible: regenerated on 25 Nov 2026, each file was byte-identical to the committed one.

<Callout kind="note" title="The Jira and Confluence part">
  The repository records that the 50 demands were loaded into a private Jira Cloud project and that pages were published to a private Confluence space, both on Free plans, on 17 and 18 Nov 2026. That state is documented by recorded snapshots and screenshots; this page describes it as it was recorded then. Reports are generated from the dataset; there is no live synchronisation, and the decision gates live in the Python model, not in Jira.
</Callout>

Plain Python 3.12 with CSV files and dataclasses: no pandas, no web framework.

1. **Generate.** `catalog.py` holds 50 hand-authored demands. `generator.py` derives status history, invoices, purchase orders and capacity rows with a per-demand seed and injects named anomalies. `ground_truth.json` is tallied inside the generator, never by the reporting code.
2. **Load.** `models.load_dataset` keeps unparseable values as `None` so the validator can report them. No row is dropped.
3. **Analyse.** `finance.reconcile` builds a commitment-based forecast per cost centre, `capacity.assess` the hours per team and month, `scoring.evaluate` a weighted priority score and four gates (information, funding, capacity, security). `kpis.compute` returns about 40 KPIs, each with its definition and the record ids behind it.
4. **Validate.** `validator.build_register` merges all findings into one register with a risk, an owner role and a due date per entry. Any CRITICAL entry makes the report state PROVISIONAL.
5. **Publish.** `reports.build_context` feeds every output, so reports, workbooks, deck and page sources cannot disagree on a number.

The Jira lane meets the pipeline in one place, offline: `jira_sync.reconcile` and `jira_live.drift` compare a recorded snapshot of the 50 issues with the dataset. The repository also holds a Jira REST v3 client with paging, retry and a write guard, tested against a mocked HTTP session.

<details>
<summary>Data-quality rules behind the 19 findings</summary>

At the cutoff the validator reports 6 critical findings, 12 warnings and 1 informational entry. The full list is in the demo.

- **Invalid amount or date.** A negative amount without a credit-note reference, or a date such as `2026-13-45`, excludes the invoice row and lists it.
- **Unsupported currency.** No conversion rate is approved, so the one USD invoice is excluded and reported in USD.
- **Unknown cost centre.** Spend without a budget is reported separately as unmapped spend (EUR 4,300).
- **Purchase-order mismatch.** The forecast keeps the ledger value and the report shows both bases: EUR 936,700 on the ledger, EUR 935,700 on invoices.
- **Missing approver.** An approved demand whose decision record has no authorised approver role.
- **Warnings.** Duplicates (counted once), spend before approval, an over-allocated team month, a forecast overrun on one cost centre, missing owners, and a status history that disagrees with the register.

</details>

<details>
<summary>KPI semantics</summary>

- **Overdue** is strict: a demand due on the cutoff day is not overdue.
- **Stale** means open, not deferred, and no status event for 14 days or more.
- **Status breakdown** counts all demands; every other breakdown counts open ones only.
- **Empty selections** return no value for ratios and medians, not zero.
- **Dates** are measured against the fixed cutoff, 2026-11-16, so numbers do not drift with the calendar.
- **Forecast** is actual spend plus open commitments plus approved demands without a purchase order: EUR 936,700 against a period budget of EUR 1,050,000, with one cost centre EUR 3,800 over. It is a scenario on generated data, not a prediction.

</details>

## Engineering decisions

### The score orders the queue; a person decides

The priority score is a fixed weighted sum of four ratings and only sorts the review queue. Approving, deferring or rejecting needs a human actor role, an authorised approver, a recorded rationale and, for approval, a gate evaluation. The alternative, letting a high score imply approval, fails on the dataset's scripted example: ORION-REQ-017 scores 82 (band Highest) and is still deferred, because its preferred option fails the funding and capacity gates.

```python
    if new_status in (c.APPROVED, c.DEFERRED, c.REJECTED):
        if not _is_human_role(actor_role):
            raise LifecycleError(f"{d.external_id}: decisions are taken by a human role, not by '{actor_role}'.")
        if not decision or not (decision.get("approver_role") or "").strip():
            raise LifecycleError(f"{d.external_id}: a decision record with an approver role is required.")
        approver = decision["approver_role"].strip()
        if approver not in c.AUTHORIZED_APPROVERS:
            raise LifecycleError(f"{d.external_id}: '{approver}' is not an authorised approver.")
        if not (decision.get("rationale") or "").strip():
            raise LifecycleError(f"{d.external_id}: a decision needs a recorded rationale.")
        if new_status == c.APPROVED:
            if approver not in c.BOARD_APPROVERS:
                raise LifecycleError(f"{d.external_id}: '{approver}' may reject or route, but not approve spend.")
```

Trade-off: the controls are enforced in the Python model. A Jira Free project has no workflow validators, so there they are documented process rules.

### Exclude and disclose; never mix currencies

An invalid ledger row leaves the totals and enters the register in the same step. The decision log records the bug behind the currency rule: an early version had a column named `amount_eur`, added a USD 5,000 invoice to EUR rows and reported EUR 9,300. The fix renamed the column to `amount` plus `currency` and made the summing function refuse a mixed list. Converting at an assumed rate would have invented data.

<p class="src">src/infraflow/finance.py:49-54 @ bf7ebe0</p>

```python
def sum_same_currency(rows: list[dict], currency: str) -> float:
    """Sum ``amount`` over rows, refusing any row that is not in ``currency``."""
    other = sorted({r.get("currency") or "?" for r in rows} - {currency})
    if other:
        raise CurrencyMixError(f"Cannot add {', '.join(other)} amounts to a {currency} total without an approved conversion rate.")
    return sum(r["amount"] or 0 for r in rows)
```

Trade-off: the conversion path with an explicit rate is unit-tested, and the dataset never exercises it.

### Twelve lifecycle stages on five Jira statuses

The Free team-managed Jira project has five statuses and no custom fields; the model has twelve stages. Each stage maps to one status and the exact stage travels as a `stage-*` label, next to labels for team, category, cost centre and review flags. All 16 JQL queries are built in code from two base strings, and the expected result of each is computed from the dataset, independently of Jira.

### Byte-identical outputs and an independent oracle

Cutoff and seed are fixed, so the CSV files are deterministic. `openpyxl` and `python-pptx` stamp the current time into document properties and zip entries; a post-processing step rewrites both to the cutoff date, so two builds from the same data are identical. CI fails on any difference in the data and text reports and prints the Office comparison as information.

## Limitations

- **Synthetic throughout.** The figures are tuned so that the scenarios occur; lead times, on-time rates and the forecast describe a generated history.
- **No live integration.** Reports are generated from CSV files, and the REST client is tested against a mocked HTTP session only.
- **The Atlassian state is a recorded one.** It was on a private site without a public link, and the record notes one extra saved filter with a wrong query that needed a manual fix.
- **Gates are enforced offline** and evaluate one demand at a time; combinations need the what-if command.
- **Finance and capacity are simple by design.** The forecast is commitment-based, hours ignore skills and task order, and working days are Monday to Friday.
- **Office output.** PivotTables cannot be generated, and rendering is checked by eye.
- **Evidenced build window.** The public history is a sanitised single import; decision log, evidence and commits are dated 17 to 19 Nov 2026.

## What I would do next

Two lessons are on record in the decision log. A column name that encodes a unit, such as `amount_eur`, invites the wrong sum the day a row arrives in another currency. And one number can mislead: the two excluded invoices have opposite signs, so the report states them gross and net.

- Run the REST path against a Jira site with an API token, so the client, the seeding and `jira pull` are exercised for real.
- Correct the saved filter noted above and align the two meanings of "stale" (7 days in the Jira filter, 14 days in the KPI).
- Add optional Jira automation for reminders and labels only; approvals stay a recorded human decision.
- Render the workbooks and the deck headlessly in CI, so layout is tested as well as structure.
